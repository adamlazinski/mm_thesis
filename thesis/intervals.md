The point of the table is the width, not the centre. Where an interval spans zero it is marked, and no claim in the thesis should rest on the sign of such a row alone.

The interval is a block bootstrap over the event series in time order; `n_eff` is the effective sample size after the events' own serial correlation, which matters because dislocation events arrive in episodes rather than independently. Where `n_eff` is close to `n` the events are effectively independent draws; where it is much smaller, the nominal count overstates the evidence. The 5 bps rows — the ones Ch7 §4 reports — rest on 49 to 91 events, and the intervals above are the honest precision of that.


## What the widths mean

Three readings follow from the table and are carried into the chapters.

1. **Part I's apparent profit is precisely estimated and that is the point.** A thirty-day interval that excludes zero by a wide margin is exactly what makes the mirage worth dismantling: the number is real, reproducible and not noise. The thesis's claim against it is not statistical but structural.

2. **Part II's intervals are wide, and the cluster correction is where the honesty is.** Treating thirty coin-days as thirty independent observations narrows the interval by about a fifth and is wrong — six coins is the replication count, not thirty. Reported correctly the round trip still excludes zero, but only just, and the thesis's basket framing rather than any per-coin figure is what the data supports.

3. **Five days cannot carry a sign on their own.** Every per-coin and per-window figure built on four or five days has an interval far wider than the differences between configurations. This is why the bridge control, where the same coin is observed twice two months apart, does more work than the cross-section of six coins.
